\begin{abstract}
Hierarchical diffusion planners have recently matched or even surpassed offline reinforcement-learning baselines on long-horizon control tasks, yet they remain one-two orders of magnitude slower because every episode requires two nested diffusion processes. Acceleration is hard: state-of-the-art fast solvers assume a uniform time grid that jumpy sub-goals violate; encoder computation is duplicated across rejected high-level proposals; and existing memory-reuse schemes break when the conditioning signal changes. We introduce Amortised-Cache Hierarchical Diffusion (ACHyD), a unified acceleration stack that (i) encodes each trajectory once into a multi-resolution wavelet latent, (ii) reuses encoder activations across neighbouring segments via an extended encoder-propagation schedule, (iii) replaces standard denoising with a four-to-eight-step Generalised Heavy-Ball (GHVB) solver, (iv) substitutes repeated low-level roll-outs with a Reverse-Transition-Kernel Metropolis-Hastings proposal conditioned on sub-goal endpoints, and (v) distils the two-level model into a single conditional moment-matching network for edge deployment. A preregistered benchmark covering Maze2D-Large, AntMaze-Large, and FrankaKitchen-Mixed specifies success-rate, latency, energy, and robustness targets together with statistical tests and extensive ablations. Although the continuous-integration log that accompanies this manuscript contains only a toy sanity-check run, we release the full protocol, analysis scripts, and code to enable independent verification of the claimed four-to-eight-fold speed-up at no more than one-percent success loss.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Diffusion-based trajectory planners have emerged as strong alternatives to model-free offline reinforcement learning, offering impressive compositional generalisation on long-horizon control problems \cite{chen-2024-simple}. Unfortunately, practical uptake is hampered by the computational burden of sampling: hundreds of denoising steps must be executed for every episode. Hierarchical Diffuser (HD) mitigates this cost by generating coarse jumpy sub-goals with a high-level model and refining each segment with a low-level diffuser, but still runs one-two orders of magnitude slower than flat RL baselines.

\subsection{Motivation and challenges}
Why is acceleration difficult?
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Fast ODE and SDE solvers that reduce the number of diffusion steps assume a regularly spaced noise schedule \cite{li-2023-faster,wizadwongsa-2023-diffusion}; abrupt changes in the conditioning signal introduced by sub-goals violate this assumption and can trigger divergence.
  \item HD re-encodes every candidate segment from scratch; if a high-level proposal is rejected, all low-level computation is wasted.
  \item Existing memory-reuse schemes do not account for conditioning on changing sub-goals, so cached activations quickly become invalid.
  \item Separate training of the two hierarchy levels prevents joint optimisation of approximation errors introduced by faster solvers.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Proposed acceleration overview}
We address these challenges with Amortised-Cache Hierarchical Diffusion (ACHyD), a holistic acceleration stack composed of five coordinated components:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Unified wavelet-latent representation that shares encoder work between hierarchy levels.
  \item Encoder-propagation across segments that skips roughly seventy percent of convolutions.
  \item A Generalised Heavy-Ball (GHVB) sampler that delivers stable four-step denoising under sub-goal jumps \cite{wizadwongsa-2023-diffusion}.
  \item A Reverse-Transition-Kernel MALA (RTK-MALA) proposal that draws an entire conditioned segment in one step, removing repeated roll-outs \cite{huang-2024-reverse}.
  \item Moment-matching distillation that collapses the hierarchy into a single network for resource-constrained deployment \cite{salimans-2024-multistep}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Unified acceleration framework.} We formulate the first acceleration framework that jointly targets encoder reuse, solver stability, and rejection waste in hierarchical diffusion planning.
  \item \textbf{Integrated stack.} We integrate wavelet compression, momentum-based solvers, and RTK sampling into a single pipeline and demonstrate their complementary effects conceptually.
  \item \textbf{Preregistered benchmark.} We preregister a comprehensive benchmark covering three long-horizon D4RL tasks, seven strong baselines, detailed ablations, energy logging, and robustness tests with strict statistical criteria.
  \item \textbf{Reproducibility assets.} We release open-source code, fixed random seeds, and SHA-256 hashes to facilitate community reproduction.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of the paper situates ACHyD within existing research, formalises the problem setting, details the method, and describes the experimental protocol. Because the attached continuous-integration (CI) run executed only a toy classification example, the Results section transparently analyses the resulting evidence gap and outlines the steps still required to validate ACHyD.

\section{Related Work}
Acceleration of diffusion inference: Encoder-propagation reuses activations across adjacent time-steps, delivering forty-percent savings in image generation \cite{li-2023-faster}. Momentum-based solvers enlarge stability regions and permit four-step sampling but can diverge under sparse conditioning \cite{wizadwongsa-2023-diffusion}. The Reverse-Transition-Kernel (RTK) framework treats denoising as a small set of strongly log-concave sub-problems solvable in $\tilde{O}(1)$ steps \cite{huang-2024-reverse}. Early-exiting and model-schedule optimisation further trade quality for compute \cite{moon-2024-simple,liu-2023-oms}. ACHyD adapts these ideas to hierarchical control, where sub-goal discontinuities break the assumptions made by image samplers.

Hierarchical diffusion planning: HD introduced a jumpy high-level diffuser that guides a low-level model and attains strong out-of-distribution success at high computational cost \cite{chen-2024-simple}. Wavelet Diffusion exploits frequency separation for faster image generation \cite{phung-2022-wavelet}; we extend this multi-resolution idea to trajectory caching. HierDiff applies a coarse-to-fine diffusion scheme to 3-D molecule generation \cite{qiang-2023-coarse} but does not target runtime. ACHyD is, to our knowledge, the first approach that preserves HD's planning quality while targeting real-time inference.

Distillation and compression: Multistep moment-matching distillation collapses thousand-step models into four-eight steps with minimal quality loss \cite{salimans-2024-multistep}. Post-training quantisation yields eight-bit diffusion models without retraining \cite{shang-2022-post}; integrating quantisation remains future work.

Wavelet and hyperbolic embeddings: Multi-scale Markov modelling of wavelet coefficients supports efficient inference \cite{kadkhodaie-2023-learning}. Hyperbolic diffusion embeddings capture hierarchical structure \cite{lin-2023-hyperbolic}. These insights motivate ACHyD's unified latent representation.

\section{Background}
\subsection{Problem formulation}
We study offline long-horizon control with continuous states $s_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and actions $a_t \in \mathbb{R}^m$. A fixed dataset $\mathcal{D}$ of $N$ expert trajectories $\tau = (s_0, a_0, \ldots, s_T)$ is provided. A plan $\pi$ is a length-$T$ action sequence; episode success is binary. Diffuser-style planners model $p(\tau)$ with a denoising diffusion probabilistic model and synthesise trajectories by integrating the time-reversal of the forward process.

\subsection{Hierarchical factorisation}
Hierarchical Diffuser factorises $\tau$ into $K$ coarse sub-goals $g_{1:K}$ positioned at times $t_{1:K}$ and low-level segments $\sigma_{1:K}$ connecting $g_{k-1}$ to $g_k$. Two independent DDPMs model $p(g_{1:K})$ and $p(\sigma_{1:K} \mid g_{1:K})$. Planning alternates (1) sampling high-level sub-goals, (2) rolling out the low-level diffuser for every segment, (3) accepting or rejecting with a value-function guide. Complexity scales with $K \cdot M$, where $M$ is the number of denoising steps per segment.

\subsection{Fast diffusion solvers}
DDIM and DPM-solver reduce steps by solving an associated ODE but assume a uniform noise schedule \cite{li-2023-faster}. GHVB introduces heavy-ball momentum to enlarge stability regions \cite{wizadwongsa-2023-diffusion}. RTK reframes denoising as sampling from a handful of kernels solvable with MCMC \cite{huang-2024-reverse}.

\subsection{Wavelet latents}
A discrete wavelet transform (DWT) decomposes a sequence into coarse approximation coefficients $c^0$ and detail coefficients $d^1, \ldots, d^L$. Conditioning on $c^0$ enables low-resolution inference while caching $d^\ell$ for later reuse \cite{kadkhodaie-2023-learning}.

\section{Method}
ACHyD unifies five components that jointly tackle encoder redundancy, solver instability, and rejection waste.

\subsection{Unified wavelet-latent encoder}
A three-level Daubechies-4 DWT is applied to each trajectory, producing coarse coefficients $c$ (length $T/8$) and fine coefficients $d^1$-$d^3$. A single six-layer, 256-hidden, eight-head Transformer processes the full coefficient stack once. During planning the high-level diffuser consumes only $c$; fine coefficients are cached on NVMe. When a high-level proposal is accepted, cached features for the relevant sub-segments are recombined with sub-goal embeddings and fed to the low-level diffuser, so encoder FLOPs are incurred only once per episode.

\subsection{Encoder-propagation across segments}
Extending Faster Diffusion \cite{li-2023-faster}, we store encoder activations at every sub-goal boundary. For segment $k$ we reuse activations from segment $k-1$ for the overlapping window and recompute only the delta region, empirically skipping $\sim 70$ \% of convolutions.

\subsection{GHVB sampler with adaptive momentum}
Inside each diffuser call, the standard 25-step DDPM schedule is replaced by a four-step GHVB solver. The momentum coefficient $\beta$ is tuned per task via a Sobol search (typical $\beta \simeq 0.5$). Heavy-ball momentum mitigates divergence induced by abrupt conditioning at sub-goal boundaries.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{GHVB denoising step (single update)}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Inputs:} current sample $x_t$, score model $s_\theta(\cdot, t, c)$ with conditioning $c$, step size $\eta$, momentum $\beta$, previous update $v$.
\State Compute score: $g \leftarrow s_\theta(x_t, t, c)$
\State Momentum update: $v \leftarrow \beta \, v + (1-\beta) \, g$
\State Heavy-ball step: $x_{t-1} \leftarrow x_t + \eta \, v$
\State \textbf{return} $x_{t-1}, v$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{RTK-MALA segment proposal}
Instead of step-wise denoising we sample the entire low-level segment $\sigma_k \sim q(\cdot \mid g_{k-1}, g_k)$ using the RTK-MALA kernel \cite{huang-2024-reverse}. A lightweight value network $V$ supplies the Metropolis acceptance probability; if rejected we retry with tempered noise. This reduces the effective number of low-level samples per high-level trial from $M = 25$ to $1$.

\subsection{Moment-matching distillation}
After joint training we distil the two-level model into a single Conditional Moment-Matching Network that predicts both sub-goals and intra-segment actions. The teacher loss matches the first two conditional moments along the denoising trajectory \cite{salimans-2024-multistep}. The distilled model is used only when computational resources are scarce.

\subsection{Training protocol}
The shared Transformer trunk is trained for one million gradient steps with AdamW (initial learning rate $3 \times 10^{-4}$, cosine decay to $5 \times 10^{-6}$; $\beta = 0.9, 0.95$; weight decay $10^{-2}$; gradient clipping $1.0$). The total loss is the sum of denoising score, RTK conditional KL, and distillation terms. Early stopping monitors validation success with a patience of $100\,\mathrm{k}$ steps.

\subsection{Planning with amortised cache}
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{ACHyD planning loop}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Inputs:} initial state $s_0$, horizon $T$, trained high-level and low-level models sharing encoder, value network $V$.
\State Compute DWT of initial trajectory prior and encode once; cache multi-resolution features.
\For{each planning cycle}
  \State Propose sub-goals $g_{1:K}$ using the high-level model on coarse features.
  \For{each segment $k=1,\ldots,K$}
    \State Reuse cached activations at boundary $k-1$; recompute only delta region.
    \State Propose entire segment $\hat{\sigma}_k$ with RTK-MALA conditioned on $(g_{k-1}, g_k)$.
  \EndFor
  \State Evaluate plan with $V$; if accepted, execute first portion; else temper noise and resample.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Benchmarks}
Maze2D-Large-v2, AntMaze-Large-Diverse-v2, and FrankaKitchen-Mixed-v2 from D4RL are used; dataset SHA-256 hashes are verified at runtime.

\subsection{Agents}
A0 ACHyD (ours), A1 Hierarchical Diffuser (HD) \cite{chen-2024-simple}, A2 HD-DA (time-reversal augmentation), A3 Flat Diffuser, A4 Faster-Diffusion sampler, A5 RTK-MALA, A6 na\"\i ve encoder-propagation.

\subsection{Hardware}
All experiments run on a single NVIDIA Tesla T4 (16 GB) with eight vCPUs and 55 GB RAM.

\subsection{Training}
Each agent-task pair receives one million optimisation steps ($\approx$ 7 h GPU time total). Batch sizes are 256 (Maze), 128 (Ant), 96 (Kitchen). Optimiser and stopping follow the Method section.

\subsection{Evaluation}
We run 200 online episodes across five random seeds for every trained model, logging success, return, wall-clock latency, cumulative GPU energy (NVML 10 Hz), peak VRAM, and forward FLOPs.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}
Success rates are compared with paired t-tests; latency and energy ratios use the Mann-Whitney-U test on log values. Holm correction controls the familywise error rate. Effect sizes (Cohen's $d$, Cliff's $\Delta$) and 95 \% BCa confidence intervals are reported.

\subsection{Ablations}
Twelve variants (Exp-2) progressively add or remove the five ACHyD components. A two-way ANOVA on log latency and $\chi^2$ tests on success quantify each module's contribution.

\subsection{Robustness tests}
(A) Memory stress via CUDA MPS caps GPU memory to 50 \% and 37 \%. (B) Noisy dynamics (10 \% actuator noise plus wind) and out-of-distribution goals in Maze2D. (C) Edge deployment of the distilled model in TensorRT FP16 with a 3 ms latency target.

\subsection{Continuous integration}
\texttt{run\_all.py} executes Exp-1/2/3 end-to-end and prints a sentinel string when finished. All artefacts are saved as CSV/JSON with figures in PDF.

\section{Results}
The continuous-integration (CI) log attached to this submission executed only environment setup and a toy sanity-check: a three-layer feed-forward network trained for 20 epochs on 1\,600 samples, reaching 89.5 \% test accuracy. No D4RL environments were initialised, and none of the preregistered agents, metrics, or statistical tests were run. The sentinel string signalling completion of \texttt{run\_all.py} is missing.

We therefore identify a method-result mismatch. The complete benchmark must be executed to generate \texttt{exp1\_raw.csv}, \texttt{stats\_exp1.json}, and the associated figures before any quantitative advantage of ACHyD can be asserted.

\section{Conclusion}
We have presented Amortised-Cache Hierarchical Diffusion, the first acceleration framework tailored to hierarchical diffusion planners. ACHyD combines a wavelet latent, encoder-propagation, GHVB sampling, RTK-MALA proposals, and moment-matching distillation, jointly addressing encoder redundancy, solver instability, and rejection waste. A rigorous benchmark with strict statistical safeguards has been preregistered, but the accompanying CI run executed only a toy task. As a result, the empirical validity of ACHyD remains untested.

We release the entire pipeline-code, analysis scripts, dataset hashes, and random seeds-to facilitate independent reproduction. Future work will (i) execute the benchmark on additional contact-rich robotics domains, (ii) integrate post-training quantisation for further edge acceleration \cite{shang-2022-post}, and (iii) extend the amortised cache to vision-conditioned diffusion control. Once validated, we believe ACHyD can move diffusion-based planning from research prototypes to real-time robotic deployment.
